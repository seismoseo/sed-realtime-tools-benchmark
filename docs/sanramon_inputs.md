**Main caveat for the later comparison.** The two DD references differ more than their stated errors.
The NCEDC DD positions of these 2015 events are real-time DD-RT solutions. LDEO v202112.1 is a full
re-inversion (2022) with cross-correlation data. For 682 common events:
* the median offset (E, N, depth) between them is (+0.17, +0.07, −0.61) km;
* after removing that offset, the median remaining difference is 0.16 km horizontal and 0.23 km
  vertical;
* inter-event vectors of pairs closer than 2 km differ by a median of 0.25 km horizontal and
  0.38 km vertical.

LDEO's own 95 % bootstrap errors are only 0.013 km horizontal and 0.014 km vertical (median). **We
recommend LDEO v202112.1 as the primary reference and NCEDC DD as the secondary one**, and judging
rtDD on relative positions (inter-event vectors) as well as absolute shifts.

### 1.1 NCEDC DD catalog (the reference named in the task)

Formats downloaded (`reference/ncedc_catalog/raw/*.html`, text in `.../txt/*.txt`):

Tidy table: `reference_tables/dd_catalog.csv`. It covers the wider box (section 2.1) with a flag
`in_box`, positions from `ddcsv` and the two ddraw error fields kept as `ddraw_erh_field_km`,
`ddraw_erz_field_km`.

What NCEDC says about this catalog (saved pages in `reference/docs/`):
* **Search page note** (https://ncedc.org/ncedc/catalog-search.html, read 2026-10-17): "The
  Double-Difference earthquake catalog currently available at the NCEDC is not generated by the most
  recent version of the software. It uses a depth datum that is referenced to the closest stations
  that recorded an event. The current USGS routine location procedure references depth with respect
  to the geoid. As a result, many event depths, especially those recorded at high elevation stations,
  may be incorrect. Users should, for the time being, obtain current, geoid referenced DD solutions
  from the catalog maintained at Lamont-Doherty Earth Observatory: https://nocaldd.ldeo.columbia.edu".
  So **NCEDC DD depths are model depths** (below the local station datum), not sea-level depths.
* **Blog of 2014-06-02** (https://ncedc.org/blog/2014/06/02/double-difference-locations-now-available.html):
  * The catalog has two parts. The "base" catalog covers 1984–2011, computed at LDEO by Waldhauser
    & Schaff with cross-correlation (CC) and hypoDD. The "real-time" catalog for 2012–present is
    computed automatically with DD-RT (Waldhauser 2009). DD-RT relocates each new event relative to
    its neighbours in the base catalog. **The 2015 San Ramon events are therefore DD-RT solutions.**
  * Update policy: "The base catalog will be periodically updated, resulting in a complete
    replacement of the entire DD catalog."
  * Not every event gets a DD solution. Magnitudes are copied from the NCSS. DD solutions are not
    reviewed by analysts (status "A").
  * In Hypoinverse format the gap, RMS, error-ellipse and phase-count fields have no DD meaning.
  * Error estimates: "Bootstrap relative location errors at the 95% confidence level (base
    catalog)", "Formal least-squares errors at the 95% confidence level (real-time catalog)".
    Neither is in the csv/readable output.
* **Blog of 2015-10-07** (https://ncedc.org/blog/2015/10/07/the-ncss-now-reports-earthquake.html):
  * From 2015-10-07 the NCSS reports depth below the geoid. Geoid depth = model depth − datum,
    where the datum is the mean elevation of the 5 nearest stations.
  * "Double-difference depths will also now (eventually) be geoid depths … We are developing a plan
    to implement this transition." Per the 2026 search-page note, this has still not happened at NCEDC.
* No version string is given for the NCEDC DD catalog; we record it as "NCEDC DD as served on
  2026-10-17".

### 1.2 LDEO catalog (nocaldd.ldeo.columbia.edu): obtained

* Why it looked empty before: `https://nocaldd.ldeo.columbia.edu` sends only its own certificate

### 1.3 What Waldhauser & Schaff (2008) and the NCAeqDD README say

Sources: Waldhauser & Schaff (2008), JGR 113, B08311
(http://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~felixw/papers/Waldhauser_Schaff_JGR2008.pdf), and
https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~felixw/NCAeqDD/README (v201112.1; the 2022 README has the same
format section). Both are in `reference/docs/`.

* **Method.**
  * hypoDD (Waldhauser & Ellsworth 2000) is applied to NCSN P picks plus waveform cross-correlation
    delay times. The CC data use 1–2 s windows filtered at 1.5–15 Hz, for all event pairs less than
    5 km apart (Schaff & Waldhauser 2005).
  * Northern California is split into 513 overlapping boxes, each needing a chain of links with at
    least 7 delay times. The NCSN catalog locations are the starting points.
  * Each box runs 20 iterations: the first 10 weight the pick data, the last 10 the CC data.
  * Events in several boxes get a distance-weighted average location.
  * The models are the NCSN 1-D models (39 local models, Oppenheimer et al. 1993), each resampled
    to 28 constant-velocity layers (para. 15).
* **Relative-location errors.** Bootstrap with replacement of the final double-difference
  residuals (200 samples) gives 95 % ellipsoids (para. 19).
  * Median 0.033 km for events mostly constrained by CC data, 0.070 km for picks only. The
    distribution has long tails (means 0.234 and 0.629 km).
  * Table 1 (whole catalog): median DD errors 0.050 km (x) and 0.047 km (z). For the San Andreas
    system region SAF: 0.039 and 0.030 km.
* **Network (NCSN) errors**, judged from repeating events: 95 % levels of 0.7 km horizontal and
  2 km vertical, with up to 8 km and 7 km mislocation (abstract; para. 28).
  * DD with picks only: 0.17 km horizontal and 0.7 km vertical at 95 % (para. 29).
* **Absolute locations.** "Systematic location bias in the NCSN starting locations are not
  corrected" (para. 35). Their 1-D vs 3-D tests at Parkfield (para. 33, Table 2) changed relative
  positions by medians of 6–7 m horizontally and 17–20 m vertically, but absolute positions by
  32–299 m horizontally and 115–877 m vertically.

### 2.2 Reading the archive format (`tools/hypoinverse_archive.py`)

Column positions are those of the "Y2000 (station) archive format" and "Y2000 hypoinverse summary
format" tables in:
* Klein (2002), USGS OFR 02-171 (https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2002/0171/), p. 99: station columns 1–113;
* Klein, hyp1.40.pdf, pp. 111–115: adds columns 114–120;
* NCEDC `shadow2000.pdf`, p. 5–6.

All three agree on every column we use:
* station 1–5, network 6–7, component 10–12;
* P remark 14–15, first motion 16, P weight code 17, date-minute 18–29;
* P seconds 30–34 (F5.2), P residual 35–38, P weight used 39–41;
* S seconds 42–46, S remark 47–48, S weight code 50, S residual 51–54;
* distance 75–78, take-off angle 79–81, azimuth 92–94, data source 109;
* location code 112–113 (NCSN writes `--` for blank);
* summary line: event id 137–146, preferred magnitude 147–150, version 163–164,
  domain/processing version 165–168, depth type 169, model type 170, depth datum 171–174 (m),
  geoid depth 175–179.

Points to know:
* `ncphraw` has **no shadow ($) lines**: only the summary, station lines and terminator.
* **Amplitude-only lines.** 4,968 station lines (HN and HH channels) have P remark " P" (no I/E
  onset), weight 4, no data source, and a "P time" exactly equal to the origin time (checked for all
  4,968). They are ML amplitude readings, not picks, and are left out.
* **S picks** are on separate lines (P weight 4 and blank P time) on horizontal components (EHE,
  EHN, HNE, HN2, EP2, …). Only 108 lines carry both P and S.
* **Weight codes**: 0 = full weight, 1 = 0.5, 2 = 0.2, 3 = 0.1, 4–9 = 0 (`WET 1. .5 .2 .1` in
  `cal5-1.41.hyp`; shadow2000 Table 9). Codes 5–9 are readings the NCSS out-weighted ("a 5 added …
  duplicate reading or … does not fit").
* **Processing**: 705 of the 706 events are "NC 05, G(eoid depth), T (CRT gradient model)". The
  first event (72536151, 2015-10-06) is older processing with no datum written.
* The crust-model code (cols 111–113) is blank in NCEDC's output.
* **Parser check.** All 706 summary lines agree with NCEDC's own `nccsv` conversion: lat/lon within
  7e-6°, depth within 0.003 km, time and magnitude exactly.

### 2.5 Things to know about the picks

* **Few S picks**: S is about 12 % of all picks (section 6), mostly weight 1–2, on horizontal
  short-period and strong-motion channels.
* **Doublets**: 19 pairs of benchmark events are less than 10 s apart, 2 of them less than 2 s
  (71097629/72536795: 1.6–1.75 s; 72539160/71097734: 1.3–1.6 s). The mainshock 72540765 follows
  72540760 (Md 2.32) by 6.3 s. Waveform windows of these events overlap. The first event of each
  < 2 s pair has no magnitude, probably because its coda is overlapped by the second event.
* 28 of the 685 events have ids in the 71xxxxxx series. They look like events added during review
  (typically the second event of a close pair) and are normal NCSS events in every other respect.
* **Large residuals**: 617 of the 25,684 picks in phase.csv (2.4 %) have |NCSS residual| > 1 s. Most got final weight
  0 in the NCSS solution. They are kept in phase.csv and flagged in phase_extra.csv.
* **Pick quality is physically consistent**: hypocentral distance / travel time gives 4.0–6.6 km/s
  for P and 2.0–3.1 km/s for S (1st–99th percentile, all 706 NCSS events). Exceptions: 2 P and 1 S outliers, and 1 S
  pick with the same time as P (72561286, NC.CBR; NCSS residual −2.17 s, weight used 0). These are
  flagged.

### 3.3 The two DD versions (`tools/13_compare_dd_versions.py`, 682 events)

Here NCEDC DD depths were converted to sea level with each event's NCSS datum (an approximation).
* NCEDC − LDEO median offset (E, N, depth): (+0.17, +0.07, −0.61) km.
* After removing that offset, the median difference is 0.16 km horizontal and 0.23 km vertical
  (90 % of 3-D differences < 0.84 km).

  structure.

## 4. Station metadata (`inputs/stations.xml`, `tools/06_download_stationxml.py`)

* **What was requested.** Every channel carrying an NCSS pick in a benchmark event (599 channels,
  464 sensors), plus the other components of the same sensor. Requests used the wildcard pattern of
  the sensor, e.g. `BK BDM 00 HH?`, `NC CBR -- EH?`, with epochs overlapping 2015-10-01 to
  2015-12-01.
* **Channel level.**
  * Bulk POST to `https://service.ncedc.org/fdsnws/station/1/query` with `level=channel`, in 12
    requests of ≤ 40 sensors (~47 s each). A single 464-line request had failed with HTTP 504 after
    300 s.
  * NCEDC answered 462 of the 464 sensors. The other 2 went to
    `https://service.earthscope.org/fdsnws/station/1/query`: NN.EMB HH? was found there, NC.J053 01
    HN? was found nowhere.
* **Response level.** The same services with `level=response`, 26 + 1 requests of ≤ 40 channels.
* **Result.** `inputs/stations.xml` (16.9 MB; merged with obspy, validated against the StationXML
  schema):
  * 1,040 channel epochs = 1,027 channels; 1,037 epochs from NCEDC, 3 from EarthScope.
  * Per-channel table: `reference_tables/station_channels.csv` (coordinates, depth, orientation,
    sample rate, sensor, service, pick count).
* **Picked channels without metadata.** Only **NC.J053.01.HNZ**, with 5 picks. All 5 have NCSS weight
  codes 6–8, so none of them would have entered phase.csv anyway.
* **Channels with two metadata epochs inside Oct–Nov 2015**: 13 channels (BK.KCC.00, BP.GHIB.40,
  BP.SCYB.40, NC.GTC, NC.MDR.02, NC.MEM, NC.MLM, NC.NMT). Coordinates are identical in both epochs
  (response changes only). tool 08 takes the epoch that covers the picks.
* **Vertical-only sensors** (no horizontal channel of the same sensor): **174 of 463**.
  * By network/type: NC EH 152, NN EH 15, CI EH 4, PG EH 2, NP EH 1.
  * Within 60 km: 42 of 246 sensors. Within 120 km: 80 of 308.
  * These stations give P picks only, and S cross-correlation cannot use them (rtDD's default S
    component is the L2 norm of the two horizontals).
* **Sensor elevation.** NCEDC StationXML mixes two conventions. We checked them network by network
  (tool 08 header; one rule per station is in `reference_tables/rtdd_stations_detail.csv`, column
  `elev_rule`):
  * (a) Channel/Elevation is already the sensor elevation, i.e. station elevation − depth. Seen for
    BK boreholes (e.g. BK.SM2B.40: 200.0 m, depth 150.9 m, channel 49.1 m), BP, NC.CYD1 and WR.
    We use Channel/Elevation.
  * (b) Channel/Elevation equals the surface elevation and Depth is the borehole depth. Seen for
    PB B0xx and NC.CCH1, CGP1, CMW1. We use Elevation − Depth; e.g. PB.B057: 14.4 − 212.6 = −198.2 m.
  * (c) BK tunnel/vault stations with Depth = overburden (BDM 34.7 m, BKS 25.6 m, KCC 87.3 m, …).
    We use Channel/Elevation.
  * For the 415 stations of station.csv: depth ≈ 0 for 355, (a) 27, (b) 16, (c) 15, other 2
    (NC.MDH1 and BK.CMAB; Channel/Elevation used).
  * Using "Elevation − Depth" everywhere would have put the BK boreholes 100–200 m too deep.

## 5. Velocity model

See `inputs/velocity_model.md` (sources, datum shift, Vp/Vs evidence) and `inputs/velocity_model.csv`.
* In short: NCSS model CON (Concord–Calaveras). Velocity changes linearly between the points
  0 / 2 / 12 / 24 / 26 km, with Vp 2.50 / 4.70 / 5.77 / 6.12 / 7.95 km/s (NCEDC shadow2000.pdf
  Table 8; NCSS `cor.crt` 2023).
* The model surface is shifted to 0.29 km above sea level (the median NCSS datum of our events), and
  2.50 km/s is extended up to 3 km above sea level.
* 684 of the 685 events lie in the CON region (`multmod5a.hyp` nodes).
* Vs = Vp/1.73. This is the ratio W&S (2008) used for S in their 3-D test, and our picks give a Wadati slope of 1.735 (442
  events). The NCSS itself uses S = 1.708·P + 0.208 s for CON.

## 6. rtDD multi-event catalog (`inputs/rtdd_catalog/`)

The format follows scrtdd `doc/base/multievent.rst` exactly. Run with
`scrtdd --reloc-catalog station.csv,event.csv,phase.csv`, or with pyrtdd's `Catalog(station, event, phase)`.

| File | Rows | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `event.csv` | 685 | `id,isotime,latitude,longitude,depth,magnitude`: **NCSS routine** origin time and hypocentre. Depth = NCSS geoid depth in km below sea level, matching station elevations and the NLL grids. Magnitude = NCSS preferred magnitude, left blank for the 3 events without one |
| `phase.csv` | 25,684 (22,545 P, 3,139 S) | `eventId,isotime,lowerUncertainty,upperUncertainty,type,networkCode,stationCode,locationCode,channelCode`. Type is `P` or `S`. Codes come from the pick; NCSN `--` is written as an empty location code |

| `phase_extra.csv` | 25,916 | every NCSS pick of the 685 events, with raw Hypoinverse fields (remark, onset, first motion, weight code, final weight, residual, distance, azimuth, take-off angle, data source), `in_phase_csv`, `reason` and the QC flags below |

* **Event ids.** rtDD reads ids with `std::stoul` into a 32-bit `unsigned`
  (`libs/hdd/catalog.cpp`: `ev.id = std::stoul(row.at("id"))`, `unsigned id`; same for `eventId`).
  A larger id would wrap around **silently**, without an error. The NCSS ids here go up to
  72,562,021, far below 4,294,967,295, so the NCSS ids are used unchanged.
  `reference_tables/event_id_map.csv` maps `ncss_event_id → rtdd_id` (identity) and gives the DD
  membership and pick counts.
* **Pick uncertainty from the Hypoinverse weight code.** Source: NCEDC shadow2000.pdf Table 9,
  "subjective error" the analyst envisions:

  | weight code | NCSS applied weight | uncertainty written (s) | picks | rtDD class weight with default `pickUncertaintyClasses` 0,0.025,0.05,0.1,0.2,0.4 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0 | 1.0 | 0.02 | 5,236 | 1 |
  | 1 | 0.5 | 0.10 | 7,012 | 0.25 |

## 7. NonLinLoc grids (`inputs/nll_con/`)

* **Recipe.** Follows scrtdd `libs/hdd/test/data/nll/iasp91_2D_azimuthal_equidist/run/`:
  `GTMODE GRID2D ANGLES_YES`, `GT_PLFD 1.0e-3 0`, `TRANS AZIMUTHAL_EQUIDIST WGS-84 37.7860
  -121.9630 0.0` (swarm centre = median NCSS location, no rotation).
* **Grid.** `VGGRID 2 1301 331 0.0 0.0 -3.0 0.1 0.1 0.1 SLOW_LEN`: distance 0–130 km and depth
  −3.0 to 30.0 km at 0.1 km spacing. The velocity model goes in as `LAYER` lines with gradients
  (section 5).
* **Stations.** 264 net.sta.loc within 120 km of the centre (`nll_con/stations.in`,
  `stations_used.csv`), each as `GTSRCE <NET_STA_LOC> LATLON lat lon 0.0 <sensor elevation km>`. The
  highest sensor is BK.MHC at 1.250 km, below the −3 km grid top. 151 stations with picks lie beyond
  120 km (`stations_beyond_120km.csv`).
* **Built** with `bash tools/make_nll_grids.sh`, using NonLinLoc v7.00.16-beta from
  `software/NonLinLoc/bin`. Run time 35 s (12:58:15–12:58:50).
  * Output in `nll_con/time/`: `sanramon_con.{P,S}.<NET_STA_LOC>.{time,angle}.{hdr,buf}`
    (528 time + 528 angle grids) plus `sanramon_con.{P,S}.mod.*`; 3.5 GB. Log:
    `logs/sanramon_nll_grids.log`, no errors or warnings.
  * Note: the 2-D `.buf` files hold two x-planes although the header says nx = 1. rtDD and our
    checks read the first plane only, as the header implies.
* **rtDD settings:** `NLLGrid(gridPath=".../inputs/nll_con/time", gridModel="sanramon_con",
  maxSearchDistance=10.0)`. In scrtdd this is a TTT profile `NLLGrid` with table
  `.../nll_con/time/sanramon_con`.

### Checks (`tools/10_validate_nll_grids.py`, `16_rtdd_integration_check.py`, `17_…`, `18_…`)

1. **Our own ray code against USGS's.** `ray1d.py` shoots rays through the same gradient model
   (direct, turning and head waves). It agrees with the NCSS TTGEN table of CON for all source
   depths 0–40 km and distances 0–135 km to **≤ 5.6 ms**, median < 1 ms.
2. **NLL against ray1d, five stations** chosen to span distance and elevation:

   | station | distance | elevation |
   |---|---:|---:|
   | NC.C029.01 | 0.8 km | 0.13 km |
   | NC.C063.01 | 20 km | |
   | BK.MHC.00 | 57 km | 1.25 km, highest within 60 km |
   | NC.HSP | 84 km | |
   | NC.HSF.02 | 115 km | |

   * At 1,320 grid nodes per station and phase (distance 0–130 km every 2 km, depth 1–20 km), the
     NLL times are **later by 0–14 ms (P) and 0–24 ms (S)**. The mean is 8–10 ms (P) and
     14–18 ms (S), and the difference grows smoothly with distance.
   * All three ray types were tested: 5,308 head-wave, 3,994 turning and 3,898 direct-ray nodes.
   * At 100 benchmark-event positions, read the way rtDD reads 2-D grids (bilinear, in the grid's
     own projected distance), the difference is at most 11.5 ms (P) and 20 ms (S).
   * We attribute this small, smooth offset to the 0.1 km finite-difference grid. It is below the
     smallest pick uncertainty (0.02 s) and nearly the same for neighbouring events, so it hardly
     affects double differences.
3. **NLL against the NCSS TTGEN table**, for a virtual station on the NCSS model surface
   (`nll_con_validation/`): 720 depth–distance points, mean +10.1 ms, max 18.5 ms.
4. **rtDD finds every grid.** pyrtdd's `NLLGrid` returned P and S times and take-off angles for all
   264 stations (528 lookups, 0 failures) with `maxSearchDistance = 10 m`. Take-off dips range
   41.9–172.2°. So station.csv elevations match the grid headers, and rtDD's map projection is the
   same as NonLinLoc's.
5. **NonLinLoc's AZIMUTHAL_EQUIDIST is not distance-true.**
   * Distances from the projection origin in the grid headers differ from WGS-84 geodesic distances
     by up to **320 m at 115–119 km**: +0.27 % north–south, −0.10 % east–west
     (`reference_tables/nll_projection_check.csv`).
   * Compared with ray times at true distances, rtDD therefore sees errors of up to **55 ms (P) and
     96 ms (S)** at the farthest stations. The error is nearly constant across the 5 km swarm, so it
     mostly cancels in double differences, but it does bias absolute residuals.
   * Same test with other NLL transforms (8 stations at 60–120 km, `nll_transform_comparison.csv`),
     maximum distance error: **TRANS_MERC 6.9 m**, LAMBERT 11.5 m, SDC 21.6 m, AZIMUTHAL_EQUIDIST

     by `tools/make_nll_grids_tm.sh` in 37 s, 3.5 GB). rtDD found all 528 grids, and the difference

   * The task asked for AZIMUTHAL_EQUIDIST, so `nll_con` stays the default. **We recommend
     `nll_con_tm` for the relocation runs**; the next phase decides.

## 9. Open points and caveats

1. **Which reference.** NCEDC DD (2015 events = real-time DD-RT solutions; model-surface depths) and
   LDEO NCAeqDD.v202112.1 (2022 joint re-inversion with cross-correlation; geoid depths) differ by
   a median 0.25 km horizontally in inter-event vectors and 0.61 km in absolute depth. That is far
   more than LDEO's bootstrap errors (~13 m). Recommendation: LDEO as primary, NCEDC DD as secondary,
   and report relative (inter-event) and absolute agreement separately.
2. **Depth references.** NCEDC DD depths are below the local model surface. For a geoid comparison,
   subtract each event's NCSS datum (`ncss_catalog.csv:depth_datum_m`, 120–421 m); this is an
   approximation. LDEO, NCSS (`depth_geoid_km`), rtDD inputs and NLL grids all use sea level.
3. **Projection.** AZIMUTHAL_EQUIDIST grids (`nll_con`, as asked) carry up to 0.3 % distance error;
   TRANS_MERC grids (`nll_con_tm`) carry < 7 m. Choose before the relocation runs (we recommend
   `nll_con_tm`).
4. **S travel times.** With Vp/Vs 1.73 the predicted S−P is about 0.2 s too short at near
   stations (the NCSS fit needs +0.208 s for CON). This cancels in double differences but shows up
   in rtDD's absolute-residual equations (on by default, `absLocConstraint` 0.3).
5. **No station corrections.** The NCSS applies per-model station delays (`con5.del`); the grids
   do not. Station elevation is handled by the grids themselves: stations above 0.29 km sit in the
   2.5 km/s extension, and stations below it sit inside the model. Hypoinverse instead puts all
   stations on the model surface and absorbs elevation in its delays.
6. **Fixed datum shift.** One 0.29 km shift is used for all events. Their NCSS datums span
   0.12–0.42 km.
7. **Picks without grids.** 937 usable P picks come from stations beyond 120 km, which have no
   grid. rtDD will warn and skip them. Set `maxEvStaDist` ≤ 120 km to keep the logs quiet.
8. **Overlapping events.** 19 event pairs are less than 10 s apart, 2 of them less than 2 s.
   Waveform windows overlap, which matters for cross-correlation and DL picking. The mainshock
   follows an Md 2.32 event by 6.3 s.
9. **Stations without S information.** 174 vertical-only sensors (42 within 60 km) give no S picks
   and no S cross-correlation.
10. **Crust model label.** It is not in the data (NCEDC blanks Hypoinverse cols 111–113). The CON
    assignment rests on the `multmod5a.hyp` region geometry and on the 2006/2023 model files being
    identical.
11. **NCEDC DD error fields.** The ddcsv/ddread formats carry no errors. In ddraw, the horizontal
    error field is 0 for all events, and the vertical error field (0.02–0.46 km) is undocumented
    for DD rows. Only LDEO gives usable per-event relative errors.
12. **Processing version.** The 2015 NCSS solutions are processing version "NC 05". The
    Hypoinverse package we used is the 2019+ version "06" set-up. Velocity models are unchanged;
    station delays changed (4th → 5th generation), which does not affect our grids.
13. **rtDD default clustering** drops 5 events with ≤ 7 P picks (section 6).
14. **71xxxxxx ids.** 28 events have 71xxxxxx ids (apparently added during review); 27 of them are in
    both DD catalogs (the exception, 71098004, has only 5 P picks).
